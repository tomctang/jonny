Population Dynamics in Endangered Marsupial Breeding

Context: A wildlife conservation sanctuary in New South Wales operates a specialized breeding program for endangered brush-tailed rock-wallabies. The sanctuary tracks two distinct age groups in its population each year: juveniles (under one year old) and mature adults (one year or older).

Biologists have established the following annual population dynamics for the program:

  • Each mature adult produces exactly one juvenile offspring per year.

  • Juveniles do not reproduce during their first year, but all surviving juveniles transition into mature adults the following year.

  • Mature adults survive from year to year without any natural mortality in the controlled sanctuary environment.

  • The program starts in Year 0 with zero juveniles and one mature adult.

Q:

  • Formulate this two-state population transition system.

  • To analyze the long-term behavior of the colony (Hints: system’s characteristic equation & eigen).

  • Calculate the long-term annual growth rate of the total wallaby population and find the steady-state ratio of mature adults to juveniles as time approaches infinity.

Resource Allocation in High-Performance Computing Clusters

Context: A data processing firm in Sydney operates a specialized server cluster designed to execute parallel machine learning workloads. The cluster scales its hardware capacity annually by managing two categories of processing units: primary active nodes and standby redundant units.

Engineers at the firm have established the following annual infrastructure expansion rules:

  • Each active node from the current year generates one new active nodes for the subsequent year through workload splitting, while also spawning one new standby unit as a dedicated safety backup.

  • Standby units do not split or spawn backups during their first year; instead, every standby unit transitions into a fully operational active node the following year.

  • All hardware units remain fully operational without failure across years in this monitored environment.

  • The cluster begins in Year 0 with zero active nodes and one standby unit.

Q:

  • Formulate this two-state computing infrastructure transition system.

  • To analyze the growth trajectory of the cluster.

  • Calculate the long-term annual growth rate (the dominant scaling factor) of the system and find the steady-state ratio of active nodes to standby units as time approaches infinity.

def back_substitution(U,b):
    """Performs back substitution given an upper triangular matrix U and a vector b.
    Currently only works for vectors of size 3 and matrices of size 3x3."""
    lst=[]
    # U[2][2]*x2=b[2]
    # x2=b[2]/U[2][2]
    lst.append(b[2]/U[2][2])
    # U[1][1]*x1+U[1][2]*x2=b[1]
    # U[1][1]*x1=b[1]-U[1][2]*x2
    # x1=(b[1]-U[1][2]*x2)/U[1][1]
    lst.append((b[1]-U[1][2]*lst[0])/U[1][1])
    # U[0][0]*x0+U[0][1]*x1+U[0][2]*x2=b[0]
    # U[0][0]*x0=b[0]-U[0][1]*x1-U[0][2]*x2
    # x0=(b[0]-U[0][1]*x1-U[0][2]*x2)/U[0][0]
    lst.append((b[0]-U[0][1]*lst[1]-U[0][2]*lst[0])/U[0][0])
    return lst[::-1]
def new_back_substitution(U,b):
    """A new back substitution function that works for any size."""
